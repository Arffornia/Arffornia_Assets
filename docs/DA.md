# Art direction research: machines

Status: **research, needs Brian's pick** (decisions DA1–DA6 at the end). Owner: agents. Date: 2026-10-31.
Review board with every render: `_work/da/board.html` (published as a claude.ai artifact).

## The brief (Brian, wave 5)

The mock machines are weak. Some parts are not physically plausible, some go through other parts,
and the style is "too sober, too modern". He wants depth, like Satisfactory: each machine looks
like what it does and is very different from the others. Futurist industrial, big multiblocks,
not only blocks. Set the art direction first, with a pipeline that catches the plausibility
problems, and only then make more models. Oritech, already in the pack, is the quality bar.

## Method

- **The same five functional programs in every direction**: crusher, smelter, assembler,
  refinery and particle accelerator. Each one keeps its layout and changes only its style, so the
  board compares style and not content. One consequence is intended: the silhouettes are nearly
  identical between rows. The silhouettes must differ between machines, and they do.
- **Built in the real pipeline.** Every concept is a `.bbmodel` generated by `tools/da/`. It is
  painted at 32 px/block and has a GeckoLib glowmask. It passes the lint (`docs/MODEL-RULES.md`)
  and is rendered by the production loader. The renders are honest about the game: albedo plus
  glow, no PBR, a block-grid floor, and a player (1.8 blocks) for scale.
- **Iterated with an independent critique.** v1 was reviewed by a separate Claude session acting
  as a senior game art director (`_work/da/critique-art-director.md`). v2 applies the actionable
  points and adds the synthesis the critique recommended, as row D.

## References: what makes each read

From play and memory. No third-party images are committed (SPEC D7).

| Reference | What makes it read | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **Satisfactory** | Each building's silhouette is its process: a squat furnace with a glowing mouth, a press, an arm bay, tall columns with pipes, a ring hall. One house palette (painted orange panels on grey structure), so function, not colour, tells machines apart. Walkways, ladders and doors give scale. Standard, framed conveyor and pipe ports. | Silhouette = function. One palette across machines. Standard ports. Human-scale details. |
| **Oritech** (in the pack) | Multiblock machines with a core and add-ons, GeckoLib-animated, detailed but still at Minecraft scale. Clean metal panels with coloured accents, and a particle accelerator built as a ring. | The quality bar: detail density per block, and animated multiblocks in our engine. |
| **Create** | Motion is the readout: you see shafts and cogs turn, and speed is power. Materials come from the vanilla palette (andesite, brass, copper, wood), so it belongs in the world. Every part has a mechanical reason. | Visible drive trains. Materials that sit next to vanilla blocks without clashing. |
| **Mekanism** | Clean sci-fi grey machines. Ports are colour-coded by type. Big multiblocks (fusion reactor, induction matrix) show their insides through glass. | Port colours (item orange, fluid brass/blue, energy amber). Glass to show the process on high-tier machines. |
| **Immersive Engineering** | Real industrial references (arc furnace, excavator, diesel generator), heavy and grounded, with big animated parts (bucket wheel). Treated wood and steel. | Mechanical honesty. Weight sits low. |
| **Factorio** | Readable from far away and from above: silhouette plus animation give the state, colour is spent on state lights, and grime is heavy but consistent. | One main motion per machine, readable at 20 blocks. State lights, not decoration. |
| **Dyson Sphere Program** | Sleek white and grey with soft light, satisfying micro-animations, and a scale ramp from small buildings to megastructures. | Progression through scale and light: higher tiers are bigger and add cyan power. |

## Directions

### A · Heavy Industry (Satisfactory and IE lineage)
- **Shape:** mechanism on show (flywheels, rollers, hoppers, stacks). Mass sits low. Square and
  12-gon parts.
- **Materials:** signal-orange painted cover panels on gunmetal structure, bare steel on moving
  parts.
- **Colour and light:** warm sodium light. Molten orange only where heat is. Hazard stripes only
  at pinch points.
- **Wear:** chipped edges and grime towards the ground.
- **Scale and motion:** caged lamps, buckets and rungs as scale cues. Motion is heavy and
  constant.

### B · Clean Orbital (Oritech, Mekanism and DSP lineage)
- **Shape:** monolithic ceramic shells with 45° chamfers, the process visible behind glass.
- **Materials:** off-white ceramic, graphite, brushed titanium.
- **Colour and light:** cyan energy lines. Orange is the only warm accent.
- **Wear:** almost none (grime only at the ground).
- **Scale and motion:** panel seams and service hatches give scale. Motion is smooth and precise.

### C · Hex-Forge (Arffornia's own: the title screen's honeycomb)
- **Shape:** hexagonal prisms and honeycomb plates, stacked tiers, brass collars, exposed cogs.
- **Materials:** dark teal enamel, brass, copper with verdigris.
- **Colour and light:** amber through hex windows, teal for energy.
- **Scale and motion:** clockwork motion that ticks with the game (step, then hold).

### D · Forge Mix (the critique's synthesis, our recommendation)
- **Structure:** A's structure and discipline. About 60% dark gunmetal, 30% painted cover panels,
  10% accent.
- **Panels:** C's teal and brass on cover panels, trims and nameplates. Hex is a brand mark, not
  wallpaper.
- **Glow, two colours only:** amber means heat or process. Cyan means power, on tier 4 and above
  only, so progression shows.
- **Wear:** light and consistent.

## What the critique changed (v1 → v2)

- **Materials:**
  - no white rivet speckle (it read as noise);
  - darker paint chips;
  - hazard stripes only on moving edges;
  - one glow language per direction (B dropped violet).
- **Smelter:** squatter, with a fat short stack and a glowing pour launder between the mouth and
  the crucible. The tall stack made it read like the refinery.
- **Assembler:** a workpiece with a weld seam on the table, so the gantry carries something.
- **Accelerator:** the ring is raised on pedestals, with a glowing rail on top. It now reads as a
  continuous ring. In v1 it read as a cluster of boxes.
- **Crusher:** the decorative ladder on the elevator was replaced with buckets.
- **Not done**, left to the chosen direction:
  - scale everything about 2× (a footprint decision, DA2);
  - a drive belt from motor to flywheel (needs a tangent helper);
  - in-game screenshots next to vanilla builds (needs the GeckoLib exporter).

## Decisions for Brian (defaults in bold)

- **DA1 Base direction**: **D Forge Mix** / A Heavy Industry / B Clean Orbital / C Hex-Forge.
- **DA2 Machine size**: **scale up: tier 1–2 machines 3×3 to 5×5, tier 3+ 5×5 to 9×9** (a player
  comes to the knee of a tier-3 machine) / keep today's 2×2 to 5×5.
- **DA3 Glow rule**: **amber = heat or process, cyan = power on tier 4+ only, at most 5% of the
  surface, judged without bloom** / free per machine.
- **DA4 Lint gate**: **legacy mocks are report-only, new models gate** / everything gates now.
- **DA5 "Final" needs**: **a hero render, a silhouette and an in-game screenshot next to vanilla
  blocks** (once the GeckoLib exporter exists) / renders only.
- **DA6 Next models**: **remake the six tier 0–3 machines in the chosen direction, one PR each,
  lint-gated** / remake only the ones on the title screen first.
